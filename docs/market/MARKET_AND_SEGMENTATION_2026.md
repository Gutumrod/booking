# BK01 Thailand Market & Segmentation 2026

**Research date:** 2026-08-28
**Status:** CURRENT EVIDENCE — segmentation direction owner-approved 2026-08-28; refresh before commercial lock

## 1. Category definition

BK01 should be defined narrowly as a **LINE-first appointment booking and lightweight service-operations SaaS for Thai appointment-based small service businesses**.
It is not primarily a walk-in queue system, marketplace, medical practice system, POS, omnichannel CRM or field-service/job-order system.

The customer job is to replace manual appointment coordination in LINE/DM plus paper/calendar/spreadsheet with a customer self-booking flow that respects real staff availability and gives the shop one operational booking record.

## 2. Market evidence

- LINE's current official Thailand materials state reach of approximately 56 million accounts, making LINE-native workflow a rational channel assumption for Thai SMB research, but not a unique advantage by itself.
- Bank of Thailand reports 2,527,436.20 thousand PromptPay transactions in May 2026 with value ฿4,663.95 billion. PromptPay is therefore mature infrastructure; this does not prove deposit booking improves attendance.
- NSO's 2024 Business Services Survey reports 2023 establishment counts of 112,996 for hairdressing and 7,589 for beauty/manicure/pedicure activities. These are category-universe indicators, not BK01 TAM.
- Official OSMEP datasets provide MSME classifications by industry/geography, but registered/observed establishments must not be treated as equal to addressable paying SaaS customers.

## 3. Primary beachhead recommendation

**Hair salon / barber / beauty / nail businesses with 1 location and roughly 1–10 service providers.**

Why this segment is the best first lock candidate:
1. The current BK01 domain already models staff/provider schedules, breaks, holidays, service durations, deposits and customer-selected/any staff.
2. The workflow is appointment-centric rather than walk-in-only or resource/job-order-centric.
3. Official NSO evidence shows a large establishment universe in hair and beauty categories.
4. Current Thai competitors repeatedly target salons, barbers, nails, spas and beauty, confirming active software demand rather than proving BK01 demand specifically.
5. LINE + PromptPay is behaviorally compatible with the intended merchant/customer workflow.

## 4. Secondary segment

**Massage/spa and non-medical wellness appointment businesses** are the strongest secondary candidate because they share provider schedules, duration, deposit and reminder needs.
They should remain secondary until pilot interviews confirm operational differences such as room/resource constraints, therapist rotation and package/course handling.

## 5. Explicit V1 exclusions

- **Medical clinics:** exclude from the first beachhead lock. Medical data, practitioner workflows, consent/privacy expectations and clinical operations can materially exceed BK01's current contract. A clinic may use generic booking, but BK01 must not market itself as a medical practice system without a dedicated review.
- **Car care/repair/workshops:** exclude as a primary V1 segment. Jobs commonly require bays/resources, vehicles, parts, estimates and variable job completion rather than only provider-time scheduling.
- **Restaurants/walk-in queues:** exclude. Queue/wait-time/table capacity is a different primary model.
- **Fitness class capacity/group scheduling:** exclude from the beachhead until class capacity, membership and recurring-session semantics are explicitly modeled.
- **Multi-location chains:** do not lead V1 positioning while current owner provisioning is one owned shop/account and multi-branch governance is not a locked product contract.

## 6. Alternatives being replaced

Ranked by expected beachhead relevance:
1. Manual LINE chat + paper/calendar/spreadsheet.
2. Another low-cost Thai LINE booking SaaS.
3. Generic global scheduling link/software.
4. Custom-built booking page or agency solution.
5. Marketplace/discovery booking — less direct for merchants who already own their customer relationship.

## 7. Trigger to pay

Public evidence cannot prove willingness-to-pay, so these are testable hypotheses:
- booking volume creates enough repeated chat work that manual coordination becomes painful;
- double-booking or staff schedule mistakes begin causing customer/service failures;
- no-shows make deposits/reminders operationally valuable;
- owner needs staff-visible schedule and history without sharing a personal calendar;
- business wants customers to self-book after hours without adding another customer app.

## 8. Adoption objections to test

- setup effort and migrating existing services/staff/schedules;
- whether customers will actually use a booking link/LINE flow instead of chatting;
- trust in deposit collection and slip/payment verification;
- LINE OA connection complexity and monthly message economics;
- fear of losing customer/appointment data or being locked into a vendor;
- staff training and role visibility;
- price versus simply continuing with chat/calendar;
- reliability concern: one missed or duplicated appointment can destroy trust quickly.

## 9. Market sizing discipline

No monetary TAM/SAM/SOM is LOCKED in this pass.
The NSO establishment counts are valid universe indicators, but public evidence does not yet provide defensible values for: appointment intensity, LINE OA adoption, current software usage, willingness-to-pay, single-location fit, or reachable conversion.

A defensible future SAM requires at minimum: selected TSIC categories × geography × operational-fit filter × digital/LINE readiness, with every filter sourced or measured.
A defensible SOM requires actual acquisition capacity, pilot conversion, churn/retention and sales/onboarding throughput — not an arbitrary percentage of SAM.

## 10. Market decision

Use **hair/barber/beauty/nail, single-location, 1–10 providers** as the owner-approved primary ICP for V1 product direction.
Owner approval was recorded on 2026-08-28. Pilot/interview evidence is still required to validate or revise this ICP before final commercial positioning/pricing lock.
